## The PRIME method

A simple way to organize preparation without turning it into random LeetCode grinding.

### **P - Patterns**
Learn the recurring algorithmic patterns first.

- [Blind 75](https://github.com/ombharatiya/FAANG-Coding-Interview-Questions/blob/main/Blind-75.md)
- [NeetCode 150](https://github.com/ombharatiya/FAANG-Coding-Interview-Questions/blob/main/NeetCode-150.md)

### **R - Resources**
Build the computer-science foundation behind the problems.

- [Coding Interview University](https://github.com/jwasham/coding-interview-university) - an extensive self-study plan for core CS and interview topics.
- [LeetCode - Which Company?](https://aviksarkar.fyi/LeetCode-Which-Company/) - maps problems to companies that have reportedly used them.
- [System Design Primer](https://github.com/donnemartin/system-design-primer) - free system-design study guide and interview practice.

### **I - Interview threads**
Use recent candidate reports to understand formats and recurring topics, but treat individual reports as anecdotal.

- [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/)
- [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/)
- [Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/)

### **M - Mock interviews**
Practice explaining your thinking under realistic time pressure.

- [CS Careers Discord](https://discord.gg/cscareers) - community aimed at internship/new-grad recruiting, including interview practice and career support.
- Pair up with a friend and alternate between interviewer and candidate; this costs nothing and is often more useful than silent problem solving.

### **E - Experience matching**
Treat every requirement in a job description as either:

- a skill your CV should demonstrate with evidence, or
- a behavioral/interview topic you should be ready to discuss.

Do not memorize generic STAR stories. Build a small bank of real experiences that can be adapted to ownership, conflict, ambiguity, failure, leadership, debugging, and collaboration questions.
